# Brief for the CFO

Analysts still build each equity memo by hand from filings, market data, and the firm's earlier notes. Compliance then reads every memo. A memo takes 4 to 6 hours. About 65% of analyst time goes to gathering. Compliance takes 2 to 3 days. Nothing produced with a model would stand up to a review, and the same question does not get the same answer from one analyst to the next: consistency sits at 58%.

This prototype drafts one memo from those sources and will not publish it until a senior analyst has passed the weak figures and a compliance officer has approved the memo. The draft, the two decisions, and the finished memo are kept, so a reviewer can see what was approved. It does not replace the two people. It does not connect to the firm's market system, research database, or ESG provider. Those stay as they are.

What we can say about the five numbers, and what we cannot:

1. **Memo time, 4–6 hours to under 45 minutes.** Not measured. The prototype was not timed against an analyst doing the same memo. A computer finishing a draft is not the 45 minutes, because the two people still have to read it.
2. **Time spent gathering, about 65% to under 20%.** Not measured. Nobody recorded how the fifteen analysts spend a day. The prototype does fetch the quote, the filing, and the prior notes itself, which is the work that 65% refers to, but that is not a before-and-after study.
3. **Compliance cycle, 2–3 days to the same day.** Not measured. The prototype stops and waits. It does not record how long a person takes to answer, so it cannot show that compliance moved to the same day.
4. **Audit coverage, none to complete.** For the two demo memos, the trail is complete: each review and the finished memo are saved, with the figure, the sentence it came from, and who approved it. That is not a percentage of the firm's output, and it has not been checked against a regulator's list.
5. **Consistency, 58% to above 90%.** Not measured. Each ticker was run once. There is no second pass scored against a labelled set, so there is no new percentage.

You would know it worked by timing a set of real memos against the 45 minutes, by scoring a labelled sample of figures against the 90%, and by measuring how long compliance actually takes once the draft is waiting for them. This prototype does not contain those three measurements.
